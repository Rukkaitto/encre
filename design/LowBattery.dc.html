MIDDLEMARCH
CH. 01

Miss Brooke had that kind of beauty which seems to be thrown into relief by poor dress. Her hand and wrist were so finely formed that she could wear sleeves not less bare of style than those in which the Blessed Virgin appeared to Italian painters.

Her sister Celia wore scarcely more trimmings; and it was only to close observers that her dress differed from her sister’s, and had a shade of coquetry in its arrangements.

The pride of being ladies had something to do with it: the Brooke connections, though not exactly aristocratic, were unquestionably “good.”
